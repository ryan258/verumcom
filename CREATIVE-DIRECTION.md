## Core idea and boundaries

**Ideas que se llevan / Ideas you can wear.** A mark observed, a choice made, a garment considered for everyday use. This is the proposed creative line, not evidence that products exist. The tension is expressive graphics on familiar, usable clothing. A quiet surface gives a louder gesture somewhere to land.

Buenos Aires is the project's stated context, not a claim to represent its streets, every neighbourhood or a generation. Specific local facts come from actual contributors. Do not manufacture slang, a founder origin story, political affiliation, workshop addresses or a shared music scene to make the concept sound local. The intended wearer is a creative hypothesis: someone interested in an expressive piece they can use without knowing the entire story. Age, income and subculture are not established audience research.

The current container stays finite: two perspectives, six garments, two conversations and one manifesto. Expand their uses and depth before adding fictional people, collections or institutions. No community club, regular event, sales operation or third collaboration is implied by these notes.

## Voice: specific, direct, with room to disagree

Spanish leads; English preserves intent and disclosure. Use ordinary Rioplatense voseo for invitations (mirá, conocé, explorá). Do not sprinkle lunfardo onto neutral sentences or translate slang word for word. A contributor may speak differently from the brand; preserve that difference with their approval.

Use concrete nouns and active verbs: tinta, capa, costura, calce, papel, trazo. Allow short reflective lines around longer, useful descriptions. Prefer an observation followed by a decision. Avoid invented adversity, purity tests, dismissive customer copy and unsupported comparisons with other brands.

| Instead of | Working ES example | English treatment |

"Lujo de barrio" may describe attention to materials, authorship and making in a future approved text. It is not evidence of local production, a price tier or permission to romanticise hardship. "Lo real" means naming the state of the work accurately, not claiming immunity from digital tools. Generated imagery can help explore a design; it cannot verify a product.

## Visual grammar and material cues

- **Brand frame:** ink `#0A0A0A`, warm paper `#F1F0E8`, acid accent `#DFFF00`. These match the current tokens. Red/cobalt/yellow belong to Sobreimpreso; black/white/graphite to En Obra. Keep collection palettes distinct from interface colours.
- **Type:** Anton for display, the existing system sans for reading, Space Mono for brief metadata. Preserve accents and ñ. Heavy display type carries expression; body copy and navigation stay calm and readable.
- **Composition:** one main gesture per surface, a clear area of rest, then a small credit or identifying line. The front and back can have different visual volumes. Do not cover every garment with every brand device.
- **Marks:** use invented registration bars, offset blocks or open construction lines. Do not borrow real graffiti, transit signage, political graphics or workshop stamps as ready-made identity. Diagram numbers are not fit measurements.
- **Texture:** comes from an identified process or is labelled as a graphic effect. Do not call generated grain a fabric close-up, rough artwork a manufacturing defect, or intentional distressing a history of wear.
- **Wordmark:** VERUM in the existing text treatment remains provisional identity material. No registration mark is shown without an owner-provided basis. A future logo needs its own source file and use instructions; this pass does not imply final logo approval.
- **Photography:** show the whole garment, a front/back pair, the print or seam being discussed, and movement where useful. Leave breathing room for crops. Caption the actual state: illustration, sample or finished item. A staged location is not automatically the maker's workshop.
- **Access:** keep body text, navigation, status and credits legible; no information carried only by colour, motion or a hover. Use physical measurements when available instead of asserting a universal fit. Show seated or standing use as appropriate to the real person and garment; do not claim adaptive clothing features that have not been designed and tested.

## 01 — Sobreimpreso / Lucía Ferreyra

Fictional printmaker, imagined Chacarita studio. Perspective: preserving local memory without turning it into decorative nostalgia. Creative line: “La memoria deja capas” / “Memory leaves layers.” The neighborhood is a narrative setting, not a claim about a real person, business or workshop.

Palette: poster red (#e13830), cobalt (#224dcd), acid yellow (#e3ef28), supported by ecru. Graphic vocabulary: three offset planes, print registration marks, fragments instead of borrowed historical signage. Marks are invented; no real neighborhood artwork is appropriated.

- **Registro tee:** ecru, relaxed straight cut, proposed 240 g/m² cotton jersey. Three-color front print; small VERUM × LF back signature. The negative space is as important as the colored fields.
- **Persistencia hoodie:** cobalt, relaxed body, proposed 400 g/m² cotton fleece, double-layer hood and kangaroo pocket. Small embroidered front signature; offset red/yellow back composition with QUEDA. No artificial distressing.
- **Testigo cap:** red cotton twill, unstructured six-panel crown, curved brim and conventional adjustable buckle. Three offset embroidered bars and a small collaboration signature at the side (side placement specified in copy; plates show front/back).

En proceso / In process: **Lo que queda debajo / What remains underneath**. Six questions in three chapters: looking again → Registro; another layer → Persistencia; reducing the gesture → Testigo. Lucía speaks about attention, memory and limits on appropriating other people's stories. Her voice is reflective and visual, grounded in everyday paper and distance.

## 02 — En Obra / Tomás Varela

Fictional metalworker, imagined La Paternal studio. Perspective: change as a series of considered decisions. Creative line: “Nada está terminado” / “Nothing is finished.” This setting likewise makes no claim about a real studio.

Palette: black, white and graphite. Graphic vocabulary: dimension lines, reference numbers, auxiliary construction lines and open angles. Diagram labels are graphic motifs, never garment measurements or fabrication instructions.

- **Cota tee:** white, same proposed base and fit as Registro. Single black front diagram with an open corner; small VERUM × TV signature on the back.
- **Trazo hoodie:** black, same proposed base and construction as Persistencia. Embroidered front signature; permanent chalk-like white back diagram. Conventional cuffs and pocket; no ornamental metal or uncomfortable construction.
- **Escuadra cap:** graphite cotton twill, same conventional base as Testigo. An embroidered white angle and detached line. Ordinary adjustable buckle.

En proceso / In process: **Dejar una unión abierta / Leaving a joint open**. Six questions in three chapters: measuring possibility → Cota; retaining working marks → Trazo; leaving room → Escuadra. Tomás speaks precisely about decisions and function. An open idea never excuses an unfinished garment.

## Experience and content model

1. `/lookbook/` presents all six garment designs directly; `/en/lookbook/` is the English equivalent. `/drops/` retains the two artist/collection cards.
2. `/drops/sobreimpreso/` and `/drops/en-obra/` are collection landing pages: artist context, palette, three pieces, interview link and the other collection.
3. Each piece has front/back SVG design plates, narrative, proposed materials/fit/technique, a direct link to its interview chapter, and its two companion pieces.
4. `/veritas/` presents the two features. Each feature has a generated studio hero, clear fictional framing, chapter links, six questions, three garment inserts, collection link and next interview.
5. The homepage leads with current garment illustrations and all six pieces; the Drops index presents collections from the same sources. Menus and all content navigation work without JavaScript. No filter UI is needed for three-piece collections.

Stable `artist_id`, `translationKey`, `drop_ref`, `story_ref` and chapter `piece` fields connect content. Shared artwork lives in `assets/images/collaborations/`; localized copy lives in Hugo page bundles. Native SVG garment plates preserve exact motif placement across views. Studio scenes were generated with the built-in image generation tool; exact prompts are recorded in `assets/images/collaborations/PROMPTS.md`.
