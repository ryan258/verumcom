An ink layer that lets the previous one show through. A line that reveals how a shape was considered. A garment that finds its place in an ordinary day. **VERUM proposes bringing those ideas onto the body.** Buenos Aires is the starting point for looking; no collection can speak for the whole city.

For now, this universe takes shape through six designs, two fictional artists and imagined conversations. The studio scenes are generated images; the garments are illustrations. They help us discuss a direction, not demonstrate how fabric feels or announce a real collaboration.

## Layers that leave room

We are interested in change that does not completely erase what came before. In **Sobreimpreso**, three offset planes let the ecru ground of Registro breathe. Persistencia enlarges the gesture across a back. Testigo reduces it to three embroidered bars.

Memory becomes a design question here: what to keep, what to transform and how much space to leave. The marks are invented. A poster, an artwork or someone else's story does not become ours simply because it is visible.

## Marks of a decision

A construction line can show where a drawing began. In **En Obra**, Cota's open angle, the drawing on the back of Trazo and Escuadra's embroidery share that logic. The process leaves a readable mark.

That openness belongs to the graphics. Construction needs resolution: a secure seam, a print that works on fabric, a fit that can be checked. The diagrams on these pieces are graphic motifs, not measurements or manufacturing instructions.

## Room for an ordinary day

A tee, a hoodie, a cap. Three familiar forms that give each collection's gesture room without letting it take over. A quiet front can sit alongside a back that reads from a distance. Clothing should allow movement, not demand a pose.

Weight, comfort and durability still need to be tested. A sample will need to show how the fabric falls, what happens when arms are raised, how a seam feels and what changes with wear and washing. Until then, materials and fabric weights are design targets.

## People with their own names

We want each collaboration to make clear who made a decision and why. **In process** follows that path: an observation, a choice, a garment. The most useful question might concern what was discarded or what still does not work.

The current voices of Lucía Ferreyra and Tomás Varela are fictional. A real conversation will need its own voice, credits and permission to publish. Names, neighbourhoods and crafts cannot be swapped as decoration.

## What we can show today

The lookbook presents proposals, with no sales or reservations. There are no confirmed release dates or meeting places. When a sample exists, we can show it as a sample, including its progress and limits. When there is a real collaboration, we will tell its story with the people who made it.

Identity grows through that work too: calling each thing what it is, leaving room to revise it and showing what changed between the idea and the garment.
